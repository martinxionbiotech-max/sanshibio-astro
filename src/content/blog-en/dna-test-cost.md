# Pigeon DNA Test Cost

> **TL;DR**: How much a pigeon DNA test costs "per run" depends on the item — paternity testing, sex identification, gene ID cards and flight ability gene testing are each priced differently. As a market reference, commercial pigeon DNA typing and parentage testing typically lands in the **tens of dollars per bird**, with high-volume sexing falling toward the low end. "How much does a pigeon DNA test cost" is a common question. To answer it, you first need to understand that "DNA testing" is a broad category containing multiple items, each priced differently. This article explains each item's purpose and pricing logic so you can choose according to your needs.

## What DNA Testing Items Are Available for Pigeons?

| Item | Purpose | Pricing basis |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Paternity testing | Confirm parent-offspring relationships | By number of individuals compared |
| Sex identification | Tell males from females in young birds | By sample count |
| Gene ID card | Build a unique identity record | Per individual archived |
| Flight ability gene test | Assess flight talent | By test panel |
| AB pigeon detection | Identify same-name substitutes | By sample count |

Different items solve different problems, and their pricing logic is completely different — you can't simply ask "how much per run." See the gene testing sub-site [genes.sanshibio.net](https://genes.sanshibio.net/) for item details.

## The Market Ballpark

While Sanshi Bio's own fees follow an official quote, it helps to know where the commercial market sits. International pigeon DNA services typically price:

- **DNA typing / parentage testing** — in the **tens of dollars per bird** (e.g., around $50 for a standard profile at established labs).
- **Single-gene performance tests** (such as DRD4) — typically **$40–50** per marker.
- **DNA sexing** — usually the cheapest item, falling from tens of dollars toward the low end as batch size rises, because it is highly automated.

These are market references, not Sanshi Bio quotes — but they give you a frame for judging whether any provider's pricing is reasonable. The pattern to note: sexing is cheap because it is one automated marker; performance panels cost more because they run multiple loci; parentage costs more still because it involves comparing multiple individuals.

## Factors That Affect DNA Testing Price

Whatever the item, the price is affected by:

| Factor | Explanation |

| Loci tested | More loci mean higher precision |
| Report turnaround | Expedited service affects the price |

## How to Do DNA Testing at a Reasonable Cost

1. **Clarify your goal**: decide what problem you want to solve, then pick the matching item — avoid blindly ordering the "full package."
2. **Test in bulk**: sending multiple birds at once is usually more economical.
3. **Ask for a full quote**: state the item, quantity and report turnaround clearly to get an accurate quote.
4. **Prioritize quality control**: choose a provider with sufficient loci and standardized processes — don't compare unit prices alone.

The editorial point worth making: price is the wrong first filter. A DNA result you cannot trust is worth nothing, so screen for locus count, quality control and report interpretability *first*, and treat the price difference between providers as a secondary consideration. A cheap test that skips controls is the most expensive mistake a breeding program can make.

## Cost per Decision: What Each Item Actually Protects

The most useful way to think about DNA test pricing is not "per bird" but **per decision the result protects**. Each test item hedges a different financial risk, and the ratio between test cost and protected value is what makes the spending rational:

| Test item | The decision it protects | Typical protected value |
|-----------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Paternity test | Buying an imported breeder on a pedigree | High — the full breeder purchase price |
| Sex identification | Pairing a round of squabs correctly | Medium — a wasted breeding round |
| Gene ID card | Identity in a future sale or dispute | Medium — the bird's resale and stud value |
| Flight ability genes | Which birds to keep and pair | Medium — years of loft investment |
| AB pigeon detection | Fairness of a race result | High — prize money and reputation |

The pattern worth noticing: the tests that cost the most per sample are also the ones protecting the largest sums. A paternity test on a five-figure breeder import is a rounding error next to the risk of buying a "champion" with a forged pedigree. A sex test on a round of 20 squabs is cheap next to mis-sexing even a single future breeder. When you frame it this way, the question stops being "how much does a DNA test cost" and becomes "what am I risking by *not* testing" — and that number is almost always far larger.

This is also why "cheapest provider" is the wrong filter. A result you cannot trust has negative value: it gives you false confidence to make the exact mistake the test was meant to prevent. Screen for locus count, quality control and report interpretability first, and let price be the tiebreaker — not the gatekeeper.

## Bundling Items: One Sample, Multiple Answers

The cheapest DNA test is often the one you are already sending for. Because a single feather submission can feed several tests at once, bundling changes the economics materially:

| Bundle | One feather covers | What you save |
|--------|--------------------|---------------|
| Identity + parentage | Gene ID card + paternity | One sampling, one shipment, linked records |
| Sex + flight genes | Sexing + 8-gene panel | One submission covers the breeding decision |
| Health + identity | Pathogen screen + gene ID | Full picture of a new or imported bird |

The savings are not just the per-item fees — they are the logistics: one feather collection, one labeling job, one shipment, and one set of results that already cross-reference each other. For a new breeder or a high-value purchase, the "full picture" bundle (identity + parentage + health + sex) answers in one submission what four separate orders would answer over weeks.

This is the right frame for the cost question: instead of asking "how much is a DNA test," ask "what single submission answers everything I need to decide about this bird." The answer is usually more items per submission, not fewer.

## FAQ

### How much does a pigeon DNA test cost?
DNA testing covers paternity testing, sex identification, gene ID cards and more, each priced differently. As a market reference, pigeon DNA typing and parentage testing typically runs in the tens of dollars per bird. Contact the Sanshi Bio team for official pricing.

### Do paternity testing and sex identification cost the same?
No. Paternity testing is priced by the number of individuals compared, while sex identification is priced by sample count — and sexing is usually cheaper because it is a single automated marker.

### Can I test multiple pigeons at once?
Yes. Bulk testing usually qualifies for discounts; state the item, quantity and report turnaround clearly to get an accurate quote.

### Can squabs be DNA tested?
Yes. Plucking feathers yields enough DNA, so squabs can undergo sex identification, paternity testing and flight ability gene testing.

### How is sex identification done?
It detects sex chromosomes via gene testing to precisely tell males from females in young birds, using the pigeon/parrot sex identification kit — see [sex identification](/en/blog/pigeon-sex-identification/).

### Why is a single-gene performance test priced differently from sexing?
A performance test runs multiple loci and involves more analysis, whereas sexing is one automated chromosomal marker — more loci and more analysis mean a higher per-sample price.
