**Mycoplasmosis**, commonly called chronic respiratory disease (CRD), is one of the most common hidden respiratory infections in racing pigeons. Because symptoms are atypical and the disease is recurrent, **laboratory testing** is the key to diagnosis and purification. The prevalence numbers are the thing most fanciers never see: *Mycoplasma* is not a rare pathogen you might encounter — it is the background flora of the racing pigeon population, and the question is whether it is causing you measurable losses.

## Pathogen and Impact

Common pigeon mycoplasmas include *M. columborale* and *M. columbinum*. They often occur as mixed infections:

- Single infections are mild but **reduce flight endurance and race form**
- Frequently mixed with Newcastle disease, adenovirus and chlamydia
- Vertically transmitted, affecting squab survival

The species matter because they are host-adapted. A Polish survey of 179 pigeons found **92% of racing pigeons and 85% of ornamental pigeons** positive for *Mycoplasma* spp., with average prevalences of **79% for *M. columborale*, 49% for *M. columbinum* and 23% for *M. columbinasale*** — and **49% of birds carried multiple species at once** ([Polish study](https://doi.org/10.1637/12018-122718-reg.1)). *M. gallisepticum* and *M. synoviae*, the poultry pathogens fanciers often worry about, were absent — pigeon mycoplasmosis is caused by pigeon-adapted species.

## Typical Signs

| Area | Presentation |
|------|--------------|
| Respiratory | Sneezing, head-shaking, moist rales, open-mouth breathing |
| Eyes | Swollen eyelids, tearing, discharge |
| General | Depression, reduced appetite, weight loss |
| Nares | Nasal discharge, crusted nares |

> Note: Silent carriers look normal yet continuously shed and infect the flock — the core challenge of purification.

## Detection Methods

| Method | Principle | Use |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| PCR | Detects mycoplasma-specific gene fragments | Diagnosis, breeder purification |
| qPCR | Quantifies pathogen load | Severity, treatment monitoring |
| Serology (ELISA) | Detects antibodies | Flock prevalence survey |

PCR is the workhorse for purification because it finds the organism itself in carriers that serology may miss or that are shedding below the antibody-detection threshold. qPCR adds a quantitative layer — distinguishing a heavy shedder from a low-level carrier, which matters when deciding which birds to treat first.

## Breeder Purification

Because mycoplasma transmits vertically, **breeder purification** is essential:

1. PCR-test all breeders before the breeding season
2. Isolate and treat positives until negative
3. Build a negative core population, culling carriers gradually

This is the only strategy that actually reduces the prevalence in your loft over time. Because *Mycoplasma* is so widespread (the 79–92% background figures above), the goal is not "treat one sick bird" — it is to carve out a breeding nucleus that is genuinely negative and defend it.

## Treatment & Control

- Mycoplasma is sensitive to some antibiotics, but medication must be veterinary-guided to avoid resistance
- Treatment follows a closed loop of **confirm by testing — medicate — re-test to negative**
- Improve ventilation, lower stocking density and reduce stress

> 💡 Tip: Blind long-term medication is ineffective and breeds resistance. Use [virus detection](/en/virus-detection/) to identify the cause first.

## The Purification Mindset

Because mycoplasma is carried silently and passes vertically from breeders to squabs, eradication is a program, not a single treatment. The core idea is to build a negative breeding population and defend it: test all breeders before pairing, treat and clear any positives, and re-test to confirm. Ongoing biosecurity — good ventilation, moderate stocking density and reduced stress — keeps the pathogen load low between testing cycles. When diagnosis, treatment and re-testing form a continuous loop rather than a one-off fix, chronic respiratory disease stops being an accepted cost of keeping pigeons and becomes a solvable problem.

## The Author's Take

**Position**: In my view, the fancier who treats CRD "when the birds start sneezing" is playing whack-a-mole with a pathogen that 9 out of 10 birds already carry — the only approach that changes the flock's trajectory is breeder purification, and it starts before anyone is sick.

**Reasoning**: First, the 79–92% background prevalence means "no symptoms" is not evidence of a clean loft; it is evidence the carrier reservoir is silent. Second, vertical transmission means every untreated breeder re-seeds the next generation, so treating sick birds alone never reduces prevalence. Third, the closed-loop model — test, treat, re-test to negative — is the only thing that converts a chronic problem into a shrinking one, and it is cheap compared to a season of underperforming racers.

**Disclosure**: This is my editorial judgment based on published prevalence and vertical-transmission data, not a claim that every loft should or can achieve full eradication. Purification intensity should scale with your breeding goals and veterinary guidance.

## FAQ

### Can CRD be cured?
With proper medication plus breeder purification, mycoplasma can be controlled or even eliminated, but persistence in the environment requires ongoing biosecurity and disinfection.

### How common is mycoplasma in racing pigeons?
Very common. A Polish survey found **92% of racing pigeons** positive for *Mycoplasma* spp., led by *M. columborale* at **79%** and *M. columbinum* at **49%** ([Polish study](https://doi.org/10.1637/12018-122718-reg.1)).

### Swab with cotton or flocked swabs?
Use dedicated **flocked swabs** for throat or nasal secretions — higher release rate and accuracy than cotton.

### How to judge mycoplasma-virus mixed infection?
Use [multiplex PCR or combined pathogen testing](/en/blog/qpcr-principle/) to screen multiple pathogens in one run, clarifying single vs. mixed infection to guide combined treatment.

### Which sample for mycoplasma?
Use flocked swabs for throat or nasal secretions — higher release rate than cotton. See the [sampling guide](/en/blog/sample-collection-guide/) for the full workflow.

### What is the breeder purification workflow?
Full-flock PCR testing before breeding → isolate and treat positives → re-test to negative before breeding → gradually build a negative core population, cutting vertical transmission at the source.

### How is mycoplasma treated?
Confirm by testing first, then medicate under veterinary guidance and re-test to confirm clearance. Pair this with ventilation, reduced density and stress management to prevent recurrence.

## Key Takeaways

1. A silent performance killer — atypical symptoms, yet steadily reduces endurance.
2. Ubiquitous in the population — 92% of racing pigeons positive in one survey.
3. Frequent mixed infections — often with Newcastle disease, adenovirus and chlamydia.
4. Breeder purification is key — vertical transmission requires a negative core flock.
5. PCR + treatment + re-test — a detection-treatment-clearance loop.
6. Avoid blind medication — test first to identify the cause and prevent resistance.
