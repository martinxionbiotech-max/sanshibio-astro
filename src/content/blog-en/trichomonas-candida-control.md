# Pigeon Trichomonas & Candida Control

> **TL;DR**: Trichomonas gallinae and Candida albicans are the two most common pathogens of the crop and digestive tract in racing pigeons, often mixed and persistent. This guide covers symptoms, spread and control.

Crop and digestive health is the foundation of performance. **Trichomonas** and **Candida albicans** are the two most common digestive pathogens, often co-infecting.

## Pigeon Trichomonas

A protozoan that parasitizes the mouth, throat and crop:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Typical sign | Yellow-white plaques in mouth and throat |
| Digestive impact | Crop bloating, poor digestion, weight loss |
| Transmission | Passed from breeders to squabs via crop milk |
| High-risk group | Squabs, breeding breeders |

## Candida albicans

A fungus that proliferates when immunity drops or antibiotics are overused:

- **Typical sign**: crop inflammation, oral white plaques ("thrush")
- **Triggers**: prolonged or overuse of antibiotics, nutritional imbalance, stress
- **Impact**: poor digestion and absorption, weight loss, growth retardation

## Distinguishing the Two

| Feature | Trichomonas | Candida |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Pathogen | Protozoan | Fungus |
| Lesion | Yellow-white plaque, easy to strip | White plaque, harder to strip |
| Common site | Mouth, throat, crop | Crop, oral mucosa |

The two produce similar signs and are hard to distinguish by eye — [pathogen testing](/en/virus-detection/) is needed for accurate identification.

## Prevention & Control

1. **Routine testing** — screen before breeding and before the racing season
2. **Rational medication** — avoid antibiotic overuse that disrupts the flora
3. **Crop management** — monitor crop-milk quality to protect squabs
4. **Environmental hygiene** — regularly disinfect feeders and drinkers

> 💡 Tip: Trichomonas and Candida recur easily. Combine routine [real-time PCR detection](/en/virus-detection/) with ongoing monitoring for early intervention.

## Crop Health Management

The crop is the first processing chamber of the racing pigeon's digestive system, and its health sets the tone for everything downstream. Trichomonas and Candida both colonize this warm, moist environment, and they are best controlled by managing that environment: keep drinking water clean and changed regularly, disinfect drinkers and feeders, avoid crowding, and resist the reflex to dose antibiotics without a confirmed cause — overuse is precisely what lets Candida overgrow. For breeders, pre-breeding screening breaks the crop-milk transmission that passes Trichomonas from parent to squab. When either pathogen is suspected, a swab submitted for microscopy or PCR settles the question and directs treatment to the right organism.

## FAQ

### Does Trichomonas recur?
Yes. It spreads repeatedly between breeders and squabs via crop milk, and the crop environment favors its multiplication. Regular testing, pre-breeding screening and crop hygiene are key. See [virus detection](/en/virus-detection/).

### Is Candida infection linked to antibiotic overuse?
Directly. Prolonged or excessive antibiotic use disrupts the digestive flora, allowing Candida to overgrow. Medicate under veterinary guidance and avoid misuse. See [pathogen testing](/en/virus-detection/).

### How to distinguish trichomonas from candida?
Trichomonas is a protozoan, Candida is a fungus — different pathogens, different drugs. Signs overlap, so confirm by microscopy or PCR.

### Does antibiotic overuse cause candida?
Yes — overuse disrupts the normal flora, promoting opportunistic fungal overgrowth. Only medicate after confirmed diagnosis.

### How to prevent Trichomonas?
Keep drinking water clean, regularly disinfect drinkers, avoid overcrowding, and test/purify breeders before breeding — these are the keys to prevention.

### What sample is used for detection?
Collect oral or crop swabs for submission. Both microscopy and PCR are usable; PCR offers higher sensitivity.

## Key Takeaways

1. Trichomonas causes 'canker' — attacking the mouth and crop in all ages.
2. Candida is opportunistic — often secondary to immune weakness or antibiotic overuse.
3. Mixed infections are common — differential diagnosis is needed.
4. Proper medication + management — avoid antibiotic overuse that triggers Candida.
5. Crop health is the foundation — clean water and fresh feed matter.
