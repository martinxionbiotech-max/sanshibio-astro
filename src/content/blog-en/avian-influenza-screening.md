# Avian Influenza Screening for Racing Pigeons

> **Short answer**: Racing pigeons are **largely resistant to highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI)** — most experimental infections are subclinical and transmission is poor — but fatal infections with clade **2.3.4.4b H5N1 have been confirmed in pigeons since 2022**, and pigeons still act as potential mechanical vectors near poultry. That mismatch between "resistant" and "not zero-risk" is exactly why targeted PCR screening, not blanket panic, is the right posture.

**Avian influenza (AI)** is a globally significant animal disease. Racing pigeons are relatively tolerant to highly pathogenic strains but remain potential carriers and vectors — especially when mixed with poultry or during cross-region releases. Scientific **screening and quarantine** underpin loft and event safety. The nuance matters: the evidence on pigeon susceptibility has shifted in recent years, and understanding it is what separates a rational screening program from either complacency or overreaction.

## Risk Awareness — What the Evidence Actually Says

The historical view — that pigeons are "dead-end hosts" resistant to AI — is still broadly correct, but with an important caveat:

- **Resistant to HPAI**: rock pigeons are classified as **HPAI-resistant** in experimental challenges, either failing to establish infection or showing only subclinical infection ([Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-01316-4)).
- **Low susceptibility, poor transmission**: a 2025 study found pigeons have **low susceptibility** to clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1 and "are unlikely to contribute significantly to virus maintenance, transmission to poultry or zoonotic infection" ([J Gen Virol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12444787/)). Their 50% infectious dose (MID₅₀) is higher than chickens and ducks.
- **But not zero**: fatal pigeon infections with clade 2.3.4.4b HPAI have been confirmed **since 2022**, and natural H5N1 infection in domestic pigeons has been documented ([J Gen Virol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12444787/)).
- **Mechanical vector role**: releases, basketing and transport increase contact with wild birds and poultry, where pigeons can carry virus on surfaces even without being infected.

The practical read: pigeons are unlikely to be a major amplifier of HPAI, but they are not exempt from it, and their role as mechanical carriers into a poultry-rich environment still justifies surveillance.

## Clinical Signs

| Type | Presentation |
|------|--------------|
| Respiratory | Dyspnea, coughing, tearing, nasal discharge |
| Digestive | Diarrhea, reduced appetite |
| Neurological | Incoordination, torticollis, convulsions |
| Egg production | Declining lay rate, abnormal eggs |

> Note: Subclinical infections show no signs and are found only by lab testing — and in pigeons, subclinical is the *default*, not the exception.

## Detection Methods

| Method | Target | Features |
|--------|--------|----------|
| qPCR | Viral nucleic acid | High sensitivity, early diagnosis, typing |
| Antigen rapid card | Viral antigen | Fast on-site screening |
| Serology (HI/ELISA) | Antibody | Infection history, immunity |

For a species that is usually subclinical, **PCR is the workhorse** because it detects the virus itself rather than waiting for signs or antibody. Serology tells you what the flock has *met*; PCR tells you what it is *shedding right now*.

## Three Screening Scenarios

1. **Event quarantine** — batch screening before basketing and racing
2. **Quarantine of new birds** — pathogen testing during isolation
3. **Outbreak investigation** — active surveillance when outbreaks occur nearby

## Biosecurity Advice

- Avoid direct contact between racing pigeons and poultry or wild birds
- Regularly disinfect basketing equipment and transport crates
- On any abnormality, promptly [collect and submit samples](/en/blog/sample-collection-guide/) rather than disposing of birds blindly

> 💡 Tip: AI is a notifiable disease. Follow local veterinary authority requirements for suspected positives.

## Why Surveillance Matters

Avian influenza in pigeons is rarely a dramatic, high-mortality event — which is exactly why it demands active surveillance rather than passive observation. Silent carriers look healthy while the virus or contaminated material moves through shared water, crates and race baskets, where it can reach poultry and other birds. A surveillance program built around pre-race screening, quarantine of new arrivals and prompt testing of any abnormality converts an invisible risk into a managed one. Coupled with serology to confirm immune status, molecular screening gives the loft owner the early warning needed to act before a silent carrier becomes a transmission event.

## The Author's Take

**Position**: In my view, the correct stance on AI in pigeons is neither panic nor indifference — it is "test the birds you move." The resistance data is real, but so are the post-2022 fatal pigeon infections, and both point the same way: toward targeted PCR screening of birds that basket, travel or come into contact with poultry.

**Reasoning**: First, low susceptibility does not mean immunity — clade 2.3.4.4b has already killed pigeons, so the "dead-end host" assumption is outdated in at least one direction. Second, the mechanical-vector role means even a non-infected pigeon can carry virus into a poultry-rich setting, which is a biosecurity liability beyond your own loft. Third, because infection is usually subclinical, the only detection that works is PCR — waiting for signs is waiting for an event that may never become visible until it is too late. Screening birds that move is the one intervention that maps to all three.

**Disclosure**: This is my editorial judgment based on published susceptibility and transmission studies, not a claim that every loft needs continuous AI testing. Screening intensity should scale with exposure — event participation, new-bird introduction and proximity to poultry.

## FAQ

### Can pigeons get avian influenza?
Yes, often subclinically. Although morbidity and mortality are usually lower than in poultry, their role as vectors makes routine [virus detection](/en/virus-detection/) advisable. Fatal clade 2.3.4.4b infections have been confirmed in pigeons since 2022 ([J Gen Virol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12444787/)).

### Swab the throat or cloaca?
AI affects both respiratory and digestive tracts. Combine oral and cloacal swabs to raise detection rate — see the [sampling guide](/en/blog/sample-collection-guide/).

### Can antibodies still be detected after vaccination?
Yes. After vaccination, serology can measure antibody titers to evaluate immunity — see [antibody titer testing](/en/blog/antibody-titer-test/).

### Can pigeons get highly pathogenic avian influenza?
They are relatively tolerant and mostly carry low-pathogenic strains, but they are not exempt — fatal clade 2.3.4.4b infections have been confirmed since 2022. Their vector role still warrants routine testing.

### How to choose between PCR and serology?
PCR detects current infection (pathogen nucleic acid); serology reflects past infection or vaccination (antibody). For quarantine screening, prefer PCR; for immunity evaluation, use serology. In a species that is usually subclinical, PCR is the primary screening tool.

### How to prevent avian influenza in the loft?
Avoid contact with poultry and wild birds, regularly disinfect basketing equipment, isolate and test new birds, and submit samples promptly on any abnormality. Biosecurity is the foundation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Mostly silent infections — signs are mild, but pigeons can carry and spread the virus.
2. Pigeons are HPAI-resistant but not immune — fatal clade 2.3.4.4b infections confirmed since 2022.
3. Transmission-vector risk — releases and cross-region transport increase contact with wild birds.
4. Three detection tools — PCR for nucleic acid, rapid cards for antigen, serology for antibody.
5. Three screening scenarios — event quarantine, new-bird isolation, outbreak investigation.
6. A notifiable disease — handle suspected positives per veterinary authority rules.
