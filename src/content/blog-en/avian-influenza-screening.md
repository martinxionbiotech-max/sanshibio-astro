# Avian Influenza Screening for Racing Pigeons

**Avian influenza (AI)** is a globally significant animal disease. Racing pigeons are relatively tolerant to highly pathogenic strains but remain potential carriers and vectors — especially when mixed with poultry or during cross-region releases. Scientific **screening and quarantine** underpin loft and event safety.

## Risk Awareness

- Pigeons often carry low-pathogenic AI (LPAI) with no obvious signs
- Releases, basketing and long-distance transport increase contact with wild birds and poultry
- Pigeons can act as vectors, spreading the virus across species

## Clinical Signs

| Type | Presentation |
|------|--------------|
| Respiratory | Dyspnea, coughing, tearing, nasal discharge |
| Digestive | Diarrhea, reduced appetite |
| Neurological | Incoordination, torticollis, convulsions |
| Egg production | Declining lay rate, abnormal eggs |

> Note: Subclinical infections show no signs and are found only by lab testing.

## Detection Methods

| Method | Target | Features |
|--------|--------|----------|
| qPCR | Viral nucleic acid | High sensitivity, early diagnosis, typing |
| Antigen rapid card | Viral antigen | Fast on-site screening |
| Serology (HI/ELISA) | Antibody | Infection history, immunity |

## Three Screening Scenarios

1. **Event quarantine** — batch screening before basketing and racing
2. **Quarantine of new birds** — pathogen testing during isolation
3. **Outbreak investigation** — active surveillance when outbreaks occur nearby

> 💡 Tip: AI is a notifiable disease. Follow local veterinary authority requirements for suspected positives.

## FAQ

### Can pigeons get avian influenza?
Yes, often subclinically. Although morbidity and mortality are usually lower than in poultry, their role as vectors makes routine [virus detection](/en/virus-detection/) advisable.

### Swab the throat or cloaca?
AI affects both respiratory and digestive tracts. Combine oral and cloacal swabs to raise detection rate.
