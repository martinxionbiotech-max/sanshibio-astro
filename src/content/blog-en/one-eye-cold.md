# Pigeon One Eye Cold (One Eye Infection)

> **TL;DR**: "One eye cold" is not a formal disease name but a folk term for one-sided tearing, redness and discharge. It is most often caused by **Chlamydia** or **Mycoplasma** infection, and may also be caused by other irritation. To handle it properly, confirm the pathogen by testing first.

"One eye cold" (one-eye cold) is a colloquial phrase among fanciers describing a pigeon whose "one eye looks like it caught a chill" — one-sided eyelid swelling, tearing and discharge, sometimes so severe the eye cannot open. So what disease is pigeon one eye cold? The answer is more complicated than it sounds.

## One Eye Cold Is a Symptom, Not a Disease

Strictly speaking, one eye cold describes a group of **eye signs**, not a specific disease. It is the shared ocular expression of several possible causes:

| Possible cause | Features | Other signs |
|----------------|----------|-------------|
| Chlamydia infection | Common; one- or both-eye conjunctivitis | Respiratory signs, wasting; zoonotic |
| Mycoplasma infection | Chronic, recurrent | Sneezing, nasal discharge, moist rales |
| Irritation/trauma | Dust, ammonia, foreign bodies, fighting | Usually one-sided, no systemic signs |
| Mixed infection | Multiple pathogens combined | Worse and more persistent signs |

## Why It Is Linked to Chlamydia and Mycoplasma

Both Chlamydia and Mycoplasma attack the conjunctiva and respiratory mucosa, making them the two most common pathogens behind one eye cold:

- **Chlamydia (*Chlamydia psittaci*)**: often causes one- or both-sided conjunctivitis with increased eye discharge, and is a **zoonotic pathogen** that can infect humans through aerosols.
- **Mycoplasma**: causes eyelid swelling and tearing, runs a protracted, recurrent course, and often coexists with respiratory signs.

The two look alike and are hard to separate by eye, which is why one eye cold is so often handled generically. See [mycoplasma vs chlamydia in pigeons](/en/blog/mycoplasma-vs-chlamydia/) for more.

## The Risks of One Eye Cold

1. **Performance impact**: eye discomfort makes the bird restless, eats less and its flight form declines.
2. **Spread**: the pathogen spreads quickly through secretions and aerosols in the flock.
3. **Zoonotic risk**: if the cause is Chlamydia, the keeper may be infected while cleaning the loft.

## A Scientific Response: Test First, Then Treat

1. **Initial observation**: one- or both-sided, whether there is discharge, respiratory signs, and the bird's demeanor and appetite.
2. **Sample and test**: collect a pharyngeal/eye swab for [pathogen detection](/en/virus-detection/) to determine whether it is Chlamydia, Mycoplasma or a mixed infection. See the [sample collection guide](/en/blog/sample-collection-guide/) for sampling points.
3. **Veterinary-guided medication**: after confirmation, medicate under veterinary guidance — never self-administer eye drops or oral antibiotics.
4. **Isolation and hygiene**: isolate sick birds; ventilate the loft, reduce dust and lower density.
5. **Personal protection**: when Chlamydia is suspected, wear a mask and gloves while cleaning the loft and wash hands afterward.

> 💡 Tip: do not just "suppress it with eye drops." Run [pathogen detection](/en/virus-detection/) first to identify the cause so you can cure it rather than watch it recur. See the [pathogen encyclopedia](https://pathogens.sanshibio.net/) for more.

## FAQ

### Can pigeon one eye cold infect humans?
If caused by *Chlamydia psittaci*, it can be transmitted to humans (through aerosols), causing fever, headache and respiratory signs. Wear a mask and gloves when cleaning the loft and wash hands afterward. Testing is needed to confirm whether Chlamydia is the cause.

### Is one eye cold always a Mycoplasma infection?
Not necessarily. Chlamydia, Mycoplasma, dust irritation and even trauma can all cause one eye cold. Pathogen testing is needed to identify which pathogen is involved — do not assume Mycoplasma.

### Will one eye cold spread to other pigeons in the loft?
Yes. Chlamydia and Mycoplasma can both spread through secretions and aerosols in the flock. Isolate sick birds promptly and improve ventilation and disinfection.

### What eye drops should I use for one eye cold?
Self-medication is not recommended. Eye signs may be only the surface, and different causes need different approaches. Confirm the pathogen by testing first, then medicate under veterinary guidance.

### Can one eye cold heal on its own?
Cases caused by dust or mild irritation may resolve on their own, but infections by Chlamydia or Mycoplasma usually do not, and tend to recur or spread — test and treat promptly.

### What if one eye cold keeps recurring?
Recurrence suggests a possible chronic or mixed infection. Run [pathogen detection](/en/virus-detection/) to identify the pathogen, combine with sensitivity testing and treat under veterinary guidance, while improving loft hygiene and ventilation.

## Key Takeaways

1. One eye cold is a folk term for a symptom, not a single disease name.
2. The most common causes are Chlamydia and Mycoplasma, which are hard to tell apart.
3. Chlamydia is zoonotic — protect yourself when cleaning the loft.
4. Test first to identify the pathogen, then medicate under veterinary guidance.
5. Isolation, ventilation and reduced density are key to stopping spread.
