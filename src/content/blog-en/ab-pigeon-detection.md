# Racing Pigeon AB Pigeon Detection

> **TL;DR**: AB pigeons (substituted birds) are a cheating tactic in pigeon racing. This guide covers how DNA comparison identifies substitution and safeguards fair competition.

"AB pigeon" is a hated cheating method — using two similar-looking pigeons to share one identity, one resting while the other races. **AB pigeon detection** uses DNA identification to make substitution impossible.

## What Is an AB Pigeon?

An "AB pigeon" lets multiple birds share the same leg-ring identity:

- Bird A races while Bird B rests
- Frequent swapping keeps the racing bird fresh
- Similar appearance defeats visual checks

## How DNA Identification Works

[Sanshi Bio](/en/about/) uses **58 loci** for individual identification:

1. Build a gene profile before the race ([gene ID card](/en/blog/gene-id-card-guide/))
2. Re-sample and test after the race
3. Compare the two genetic profiles
4. Mismatch proves substitution

DNA is each pigeon&rsquo;s inborn, unforgeable identity.

## FAQ

### Does AB pigeon detection require a pre-race profile?
Yes. Establish a 58-locus profile before the race, then re-sample winners for comparison.

### How is AB-pigeon testing done?
Sample and profile racers pre-race, then re-sample winners post-race — a genotype mismatch indicates substitution.

### Can leg rings prevent AB pigeons?
Rings can be swapped or duplicated. Genetic identity based on DNA is unforgeable and far more reliable.

## Key Takeaways

1. AB pigeon is substitution fraud — replacing the racing bird with a same-name, same-ring double.
2. DNA comparison catches it — genetic identity cannot be forged.
3. Pre-race identity verification — sample before basketing, compare after the race.
4. Works with gene ID cards — one builds identity, the other verifies it.
5. Protects race fairness — eliminates 'look-alike' substitution.
