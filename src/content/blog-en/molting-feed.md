# Pigeon Molting Feed Ratio

> **TL;DR**: Molting is one of the highest-nutrition stages of a pigeon's year, and new feather quality directly affects later flight and racing. The core idea of molt feed is "protein for feathers, enough energy, complete minerals" — the ratio adjusts by individual and molt stage; there is no one-size-fits-all formula.

Molt feed, mixed right, makes molting "like swapping in a sharp knife"; mixed wrong, it leaves new feathers sparse and the bird drained. There is no standard answer to the "pigeon molting feed ratio," but the logic is clear. This article explains molting's nutrition needs and ratio essentials so you can adjust scientifically.

## How Nutrition Needs Change During Molting

Molting means a full-body feather replacement, consuming far more nutrition than usual:

| Nutrient | Role during molt | Demand change |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| Protein | Main raw material of feathers (keratin) | Significantly increased |
| Energy | Maintains metabolism and body condition | Kept sufficient |
| Minerals | Shaft and vane structure and strength | Clearly increased |
| Vitamins | Support metabolism and feather development | Needs supplement |

Feathers are mainly protein, so protein demand rises during molt — the core of ratio adjustment.

## Nutrition Mixing Logic for Molt Feed

Below is a **reference ratio range** for understanding the mixing direction, not a fixed formula:

| Feed category | Common ingredients | Reference ratio range | Role |
|----------|--------------|--------------|------|
| Energy | Corn, sorghum, paddy and other grains | About 50%–60% | Provides base energy |
| Protein | Peas, lentils and other legumes | About 25%–35% | Supplies raw material for feather growth |
| Fat | Oil seeds (e.g. rapeseed, sunflower seed) | About 5%–10% | Supplies essential fatty acids and feather sheen |
| Minerals | Grit, oyster shell powder, mineral supplements | Provided separately, free choice | Shaft structure and strength |

> ⚠️ Note: The above is only a **reference ratio range**. Ages, breeds, molt stages, health and local ingredients vary greatly — adjust to the bird's actual condition and never mechanically copy a fixed ratio.

## Adjust Dynamically by Molt Stage

Molting is not uniform throughout; fine-tune by stage:

1. **Early molt** — gradually raise the protein share to stock materials for feather growth
2. **Peak molt** — keep protein and mineral supply up to ensure new feather quality
3. **Late molt** — let the energy share rise to help restore body condition

Adjust based on the bird's actual condition: watch droppings, feed intake, feather growth speed and mental state.

## Common Molt Management Mistakes

| Mistake | Harm |
|------|------|
| Too "plain" a diet | Insufficient protein leaves new feathers sparse and fragile |
| Overloading energy feed | Energy surplus makes birds fat and blocks molting |
| Neglecting minerals | Soft shafts, easy breakage, poor sheen |
| Frequent stress during molt | Stress interrupts molting and hurts feather quality |

> 💡 Tip: Health directly affects feather quality during molt, and feather quality is tied to flight ability. Pair [flight ability gene testing](/en/flight-ability/) to understand feather-related genetic potential and support scientific feeding.

## The Value of Health Testing During Molt

During molt, pigeons face greater physiological stress and latent health issues tend to surface. Around molting, pay attention to:

- Pathogen screening: immunity fluctuates during molt, so use [virus detection](/en/virus-detection/) to rule out latent infection
- Parasite control: deworm internally and externally before molting so parasites don't drain nutrition
- Health monitoring: the sub-site [pathogens.sanshibio.net](https://pathogens.sanshibio.net/) provides racing pigeon health testing to back molt management

Health is the "foundation" on which feed works — a sick bird cannot grow good feathers no matter how good the ratio.

## FAQ

### What is most taboo during molt?
Frequent stress and nutritional imbalance. Stress (loft changes, transport, fright) interrupts molting, while imbalance leaves new feathers sparse and fragile. Keep the environment stable and nutrition balanced.

### How much protein is right during molt?
Protein feed (mainly legumes) sits in a reference ratio of about 25%–35%, slightly higher at peak molt. Adjust to the bird's age, breed and condition.

### Do I need extra calcium during molt?
Mineral demand rises during molt, so grit and oyster shell powder should be freely available to support shaft structure and strength. For specific amounts, follow product instructions or consult a vet.

### Can I train during molt?
Pigeons are under greater stress during molt, so prefer moderate free flying — avoid high-intensity training and stress to protect feather quality and body condition.

### Can I keep the same feed ratio throughout molt?
Not recommended. Early, peak and late molt have different nutrition needs; adjust the ratio dynamically to the bird's condition.

### Do I need health testing during molt?
Recommended. Immunity fluctuates during molt and latent infections surface easily; screen with pathogen testing and deworm so nutrition converts to quality feathers.

## Key Takeaways

1. Protein demand rises sharply during molt — the core of ratio adjustment.
2. Reference ratios are directional guidance only; adjust flexibly per individual.
3. Adjust dynamically by molt stage instead of one ratio throughout.
4. Minerals and vitamins matter and directly affect feather quality.
5. Screen health and deworm before molt so nutrition actually lands.
