# Young Pigeon Sexing

> **TL;DR**: DNA sexing accurately determines squab gender, solving the problem of visually indistinguishable sexes. This guide covers the principle and applications.

"Is this pigeon male or female?" — a question every fancier faces. Traditional visual judgment has a high error rate; **gene sexing** gives a precise answer even in young birds.

## Limits of Traditional Methods

| Traditional Method | Limitation |
|--------------------|-----------|
| Visual observation | Small sex differences, subjective |
| Behavior | Unreliable, personality-dependent |
| Body size | Needs adulthood |
| Vent inspection | High skill, error-prone |

## How Gene Sexing Works

Birds are ZZ (male) and ZW (female). Gene testing detects sex-chromosome-specific loci:

- **Male (ZZ)**: two Z-specific loci
- **Female (ZW)**: Z and W-specific loci

In [Sanshi Bio](/en/about/) testing, the sex locus is included in the **58 SNP loci**, so it can be done together with [flight ability testing](/en/flight-ability/).

## FAQ

### How accurate is gene sexing?
Extremely accurate (near 100%), far higher than visual or vent inspection.

### How accurate is DNA sexing?
Molecular detection of sex-chromosome genes (e.g., CHD1) reaches near-100% accuracy, far exceeding visual judgment.

### At what age can pigeons be sexed?
From hatching onward. Feather or blood DNA enables accurate early sexing.

## Key Takeaways

1. Squabs are hard to sex visually — DNA sexing is accurate and reliable.
2. Based on sex-chromosome genes — the avian ZW sex-determination system.
3. Feather testing — no blood needed, test squabs early.
4. Three applications — breeding pairs, race classification, flock management.
5. Near-100% accuracy — molecular sexing vastly outperforms visual judgment.
