## Where the Money Actually Goes

A paternity test fee is not a single line item — it bundles four real costs, and knowing them helps you judge whether a quote is honest or suspicious:

1. **Consumables and reagents** — DNA extraction reagents, polymerase, primers and probes, plus the disposables (plates, tips, tubes). This is the per-sample floor.
2. **Labor and analysis** — each sample must be extracted, amplified, genotyped and scored by a qualified technician, then reviewed. This is the dominant cost in any reputable lab.
3. **Quality control** — every batch runs positive and negative controls, and ambiguous results are re-run. Cheap labs skip this; that is exactly what you are paying to avoid.
4. **Reporting and support** — the written conclusion plus a human who can explain it, which matters when you are about to commit to a breeding decision.

A quote that looks "too cheap" is usually cheap because it has quietly removed link 3 or 4 — the parts that make the result trustworthy.

## The Complete Paternity Testing Process

Understanding the process clarifies what the fee actually pays for:

1. **Consult and order** — confirm the individuals (single/dual parent) and items, and get a quote.
2. **Feather sampling** — pluck 4–6 breast feathers from the offspring and suspected parents separately, following standardized sampling.
3. **Laboratory analysis** — DNA extraction plus 58-locus parentage comparison.
4. **Report issuance** — a conclusion including the parentage probability and interpretation.

## Is a Paternity Test Worth the Money?

"What does it cost" is really asking "is it worth it." The value of a paternity test lies in:

| Scenario | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Bloodline certification | Verify champion bloodline authenticity; stop forged pedigree papers |
| Breeder import | Verify parentage before spending big on an import, reducing risk |
| Breeding management | Confirm pairing results; prevent accidental cross-breeding |
| Race verification | Verify racing pigeons' bloodlines to protect fairness |

Do the arithmetic honestly. A serious breeder import routinely costs hundreds or thousands; a paternity test sits in the **tens of dollars per bird**. That means the test is typically a **single-digit percentage** of the purchase price of the bird it is protecting — a cheap insurance policy against buying a "champion" whose paper pedigree is fiction. The more valuable the bloodline, the more lopsided that ratio becomes in favor of testing.

One caveat worth stating plainly: the test's value depends on the confidence it delivers. A **dual-parent** comparison yields a far higher combined parentage probability than a **single-parent** one for essentially the same per-bird effort, so when money is the concern, spending on the *right* test design beats spending on the cheapest one.

## Reading a Quote: The Questions That Matter

Because paternity testing has no flat price, the difference between a good deal and a bad one shows up in *what you ask*, not the headline number. Before accepting any quote, get answers to four questions:

1. **How many loci, and which ones?** More loci is not automatically better — it is about informativeness. Ask whether the panel's loci are documented and how the lab validates them. A 58-locus panel with well-chosen SNPs outperforms a larger panel of low-information markers.
2. **What is the quality-control policy?** Does every batch include positive and negative controls? What happens to ambiguous results — are they re-run or silently reported? The answer tells you whether the lab is protecting you from false results or just selling speed.
3. **What exactly does the report contain?** A credible report states the combined parentage probability *and* the assumptions behind it (single-parent vs dual-parent, prior probability). A report that prints "99.99%" with no method notes is a red flag.
4. **What is included beyond the raw result?** Report interpretation and breeding advice are part of the value — a bare number you cannot act on is only half a service.

Asking these four questions does two things at once: it filters out the providers who cannot answer them, and it signals to a serious lab that you know what you are paying for. Both outcomes save you money in the long run.

## FAQ

### How much does a pigeon paternity test cost?
The fee is not fixed — it varies with the number of individuals, loci and report turnaround, so contact the Sanshi Bio team for official pricing. Commercially, pigeon DNA typing and parentage testing typically land in the tens of dollars per bird.

### Do I need samples from both parents?
Ideally yes, for a more reliable result; single-parent testing is also possible but produces a weaker combined paternity index. See [paternity testing](/en/paternity/).

### How accurate is a paternity test?
Based on SNP-locus parentage comparison, the accuracy of excluding non-parents exceeds 99.9% — and a "99.99%" confirmation corresponds to a Combined Paternity Index of roughly 10,000. See the [paternity test guide](/en/blog/paternity-test-guide/) for the math.

### Does expedited reporting cost extra?
Report turnaround affects the fee, and expedited service usually requires separate confirmation. Final pricing follows the official quote — [contact us](/en/contact/) to confirm.

### Should I do a paternity test and a gene ID card together?
It is recommended. The gene ID card files each pigeon, and the paternity test confirms relationships; together they form a complete bloodline traceability system — see the [gene ID card](/en/blog/gene-id-card-guide/).

### Is sampling a hassle? Do I need to draw blood?
No blood is needed. Plucking 4–6 breast feathers yields enough DNA, and squabs can be tested too. See the [feather sampling guide](/en/blog/feather-sampling-guide/) for the standardized method.

### Why is a cheap quote suspicious?
A reputable result bundles reagents, labor, quality-control control runs and report support. Quotes that undercut the market usually do so by skipping quality control or interpretation — the exact things that make a result trustworthy.
