# Racing Pigeon Salmonella (Paratyphoid)

> **TL;DR**: Salmonella (paratyphoid) is a common bacterial disease in racing pigeons with zoonotic risk. This guide covers symptoms, transmission and control, emphasizing molecular detection for early diagnosis.

**Salmonella** infection, called "paratyphoid" in pigeons, is a common bacterial disease and also a **zoonotic pathogen** — it can infect humans.

## Clinical Signs

- **Articular form**: swollen wing and leg joints, lameness
- **Enteric form**: persistent diarrhea, weight loss, ruffled feathers
- **Neurological form**: head tilt, loss of balance
- **Reproductive**: reduced egg production, weak squabs

## Zoonotic Risk

Salmonella can infect humans through contact with sick birds or contaminated environments. Always wash hands after handling, keep the loft clean and ventilated.

## FAQ

### Can Salmonella infect humans?
Yes. It is zoonotic — practice hygiene after handling birds and keep the loft clean.

### Can Salmonella infection be cured?
Proper antibiotics can control it, but follow the full course and do sensitivity testing to avoid resistance.

### Which sample for Salmonella?
Cloacal swabs or feces; tissue samples may be used for joint or neurological signs.

## Key Takeaways

1. A zoonotic pathogen — practice hygiene when handling sick birds.
2. Vertical transmission risk — infected breeders can pass it via eggs.
3. Diverse symptoms — digestive, joint and neurological signs.
4. PCR enables early diagnosis — faster and more sensitive than culture.
5. Breeder purification + disinfection — control it at the source.
