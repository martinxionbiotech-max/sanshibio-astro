**Short answer:** There is no universal “day 7 do this, day 21 do that” vaccine calendar for racing pigeons. Vaccine type, age, maternal antibodies, local disease, loft history and race schedule all change the programme. Copying another loft's schedule can waste vaccine and create false confidence.

## 1. Vaccination is not “inject and forget”

Protection depends on:

- whether the vaccine matches the circulating pathogen
- the bird's age and maternal antibody level
- health at the time of vaccination
- cold-chain, storage and administration
- whether a booster is needed
- exposure pressure and biosecurity

A vaccine does not replace isolation, hygiene or testing, and it does not guarantee that infection cannot occur.

## 2. The reasoning: why another loft's schedule may fail

### Maternal antibodies can interfere

Young pigeons receive antibodies from the hen. These may protect early, but they can also neutralise a vaccine and weaken the response. The timing of maternal-antibody decline differs by loft and by the hen's history.

### Vaccine type sets the basic programme

Inactivated, live and vector vaccines have different immune profiles and booster requirements. “Vaccinate every X days” means little without the vaccine type and label.

### Health decides whether to vaccinate now

Fever, acute infection, severe stress or immunosuppression can reduce the response or worsen the situation. Assess the group before vaccinating rather than following a calendar blindly.

### Training and racing create timing constraints

Vaccination needs observation and immune development. Injecting immediately before intense training or a race may affect form. The programme should be planned with the season, not added at the last minute.

## 3. What follows: questions for the veterinarian

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which pathogens are locally relevant? | Decides whether vaccination is justified |
| What vaccine type and label? | Decides primary course and boosters |
| Age and maternal-antibody status? | Decides timing |
| Current health? | Decides whether to postpone |
| Previous vaccination record? | Decides booster vs restart |
| Race and training plan? | Decides the window |
| Cold-chain and batch record? | Decides whether the dose was viable |
| Adverse-event plan? | Decides observation and reporting |

## 4. Editorial take

I would not give a fancier a “universal vaccine calendar.” A defensible plan is:

- built by a veterinarian around local risk and loft history
- integrated with testing, quarantine and biosecurity
- recorded by vaccine, batch, date, age and reaction
- treated as risk management, not an insurance policy

If a page gives exact days without asking about vaccine type, local disease and bird health, I would not treat it as an executable protocol.

## 5. Boundaries

This explains how vaccination programmes are designed; it does not provide brands, doses or a specific schedule. Follow the product label and a licensed veterinarian. Vaccine availability and registration differ by country.
