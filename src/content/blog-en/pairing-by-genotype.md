**Short answer:** Pairing by genotype is not the act of putting two “good” markers together. It is the management of a breeding goal. Without a goal, a genotype report produces combinations that look precise but cannot be validated.

## 1. Set the goal before choosing the birds

Three questions come first:

1. **What type are you trying to produce?** Sprint, endurance or a middle-distance all-rounder?
2. **What is the weak point in your current population?** Endurance, homing, health, body type or diversity?
3. **How long can you wait for evidence?** One season, two seasons or multiple generations?

Without those answers, “good genes” simply hands the decision to a report instead of to the breeding plan.

## 2. The reasoning: why good × good is not automatically better

### Genes are not independent points

Two birds can both carry a “favourable” version at the same marker; their offspring may inherit a useful combination or an unfavourable one. Markers interact with other markers and with the genetic background. Adding them as independent points overstates predictability.

### Breeding goals can conflict

Speed, endurance, recovery and body type may trade off. A sprint-type paired with a distance-type does not automatically produce “both”; it often produces an intermediate type, or birds that diverge by race distance. Decide whether you want an all-rounder or a specialist before pairing.

### Health and diversity are constraints

A closed population can lose diversity even when individual markers look good. A genotype report does not replace a pedigree, health record or population-structure review.

## 3. What follows: a pairing checklist

| Check | Question | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | What problem should this pair solve? | Write the goal first |
| Complementarity | Do the weaknesses complement each other? | Do not pair the same weakness |
| Diversity | Is the pair too closely related? | Introduce unrelated blood or delay |
| Health | Are both health records stable? | Test/isolate before pairing |
| Evidence | Is there race, offspring or pedigree evidence? | Start as a small trial |
| Verification | Can offspring be recorded and compared? | Build pair and offspring records |

## 4. Editorial take

I do not like genotype pairing presented as a recipe.

- Use the report for **direction**, not as a command to pair.
- Use pedigree and population structure to avoid inbreeding.
- Use health records to avoid pairing known problems.
- Use offspring performance to validate the pair, not the report itself.

A pair that looks perfect on paper but has unstable health, a close pedigree and no offspring record should be a small trial, not an immediate expansion.

## 5. Boundaries

This is a breeding framework, not a specific pairing prescription or veterinary advice. Strain, race distance and loft goals change the answer. Genotypes provide probability and direction; the pair must be validated by offspring records.
